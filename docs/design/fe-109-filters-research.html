Stores

Desktop images: top left = first screen, top right = three screens down, bottom left = 250 px back up, bottom right = filters opened (or the top again when the filters are already visible). Phone images: the same four, left to right.

1. Article

article.com/browse/12/sofas-sofas (US, direct to consumer)

  • Where: desktop left sidebar "Sort & Filter", every facet open, counts next to options.
  • Sort: radio list at the top of the sidebar, no separate control.
  • On scroll: desktop sidebar scrolls away; phone keeps "Sofas & Loveseats + FILTERS" stuck under the header, which hides on scroll down and returns on scroll up.
  • Apply: instant; the phone drawer closes with "Done".
  • Ranges: price slider with two typed inputs.
  • Empty / loading: list fades while it reloads.

Phone bar is clean and always there. Desktop loses every filter after the first screen, which is the thing to avoid.

2. Castlery

castlery.com/au/sofas/all-sofas

  • Where: desktop sidebar with "Hide filters", Quick ship toggle on top, facets open.
  • Sort: "Sort by" dropdown top right of the grid.
  • On scroll: desktop sidebar is a tall sticky column; phone: "Filters | Sort by" sticks at the very top.
  • Apply: desktop instant; phone sheet has "426 results available" with Reset / Apply.
  • Ranges / colour: Length (cm) slider 50-490; round colour swatches with no names.
  • Noise: newsletter popup and a side tab cover the grid.

Closest to what Elme needs: size in cm, swatches, live count on the phone. The bar sits at the top of the phone screen, which is hard to reach with the thumb.

3. IKEA

ikea.com/au/en/cat/sofas-fu003

  • Where: desktop sticky column of closed accordions (Sort, Number of seats, Type, Colour, Price, Size ...); "Number of seats" also promoted as pills with counts above the grid.
  • Sort: first item in the facet column / first pill on the phone.
  • On scroll: the column and the phone pill row never leave the screen.
  • Apply: instant.
  • Phone: each pill opens a bottom sheet with just that facet; "All filters" opens "Filter and sort".
  • Applied: shown as pills above the list.

Best at "filters always one tap away" on both sizes. Promoted pills with counts are the fastest single filter. Closed accordions hide the options until clicked.

4. Pottery Barn

potterybarn.com.au/furniture/living-room-furniture/sofas

  • Where: desktop sidebar ("Hide filters") with counts, plus a top bar with In stock / On sale toggles, product count and Sort.
  • On scroll: the top bar is fixed and the sidebar sticks under it: everything stays reachable.
  • Phone: a 151 px sticky block (count, two toggles, Filters, Sort) - almost a fifth of the screen.
  • Apply: instant; the phone drawer closes with "Close".
  • Colour: dots with names and counts in a list.
  • Stock: "In stock" promoted as a one-tap toggle.

The desktop pattern (sticky bar with stock toggle, count and Sort over a sticky sidebar) suits a B2B buyer. The phone block is too tall.

5. West Elm AU

westelm.com.au/sofa-modulars-sc

  • Where: same Williams-Sonoma platform as Pottery Barn: sidebar, sticky bar "On sale | In stock | 1 - 72 of 146 products | Sort".
  • Facets: Inventory availability first (In stock, Pre-order, Made to order), then Category, Product type, Price.

Puts availability first, which is the right order for a buyer who needs stock now.

6. Coco Republic

cocorepublic.com.au/sofas

  • Where: one row of dropdowns above the grid: Filter (135), Colour, Material, Price, Brand, Sale switch, Availability; Sort on the right.
  • On scroll: nothing sticks, on either size.
  • Phone: full-screen sheet, closed accordions, "Filter items 135" + Reset.
  • Look: calm and on-brand; full-width grid.

Nice look, but after one screen the buyer has to scroll back up to change anything.

7. Freedom

freedom.com.au/sofas-and-armchairs/c/all-sofas

  • Where: sticky compact header, sticky "Filter | 1258 products | Sort by" bar, sticky column of closed accordions (Product type, Price, Brand, Size, Colour, Features, Dimensions ...).
  • Phone: "Filter & Sort | 1258 products" sticks under the header; drawer has Sort inside and "Show 1258 results".
  • Apply: desktop instant, phone batch with live count.
  • Ranges: a Dimensions facet (like our Width / Depth).

Everything is reachable at every scroll position. Dense, and the closed accordions cost a click per facet.

8. GlobeWest (trade wholesaler)

globewest.com.au/indoor/furniture/sofas - an Australian B2B furniture supplier, the closest peer to Elme

  • Where: sub-category tiles, then a row of dropdowns (Price, Colour, Sofa family, Shape, Size), a "Filters" toggle that opens a sidebar (Collection, Category, Stock status, Price, Colour ...), Sort on the right.
  • On scroll: desktop nothing sticks; phone "Browse by | New arrivals" sticks.
  • Cards: W x D x H in mm and In stock / ETA date on every card.
  • Phone: sheet with "Apply filters".

Shows what trade buyers look at (size and stock on the card). Two filter UIs on one page (dropdown row and sidebar) is confusing, and nothing sticks on desktop.

Baymard guidance

  • Be careful with horizontal filtering toolbars: dropdown bars hide the options and counts until clicked, and stop working past 6-8 filter types; "All filters" buttons get overlooked. 24% of sites use them.
  • Display applied filters in an overview: users need one place to confirm and remove what is applied; 28% of sites have none.
  • Allow combining filter values: several values in one facet (OR) - we already do this.
  • Promote important filters: put the most decisive filters at the top of the list as well as in the normal place; 61% of sites don't.
  • Slider interfaces: price / size sliders were misread by more than half of test users; typed From / To inputs are the reliable part (we use inputs, D2).

What this means for Elme

  • We have nine facets (Colour, Material, Type, Style, Features, Seats, Width, Depth, In stock), six with values on Furniture. That is at the limit of a dropdown bar and comfortable for a sidebar.
  • The stores that feel fastest keep filters, product count and Sort reachable at every scroll position (IKEA, Freedom, Pottery Barn). The ones that let them scroll away (Article desktop, Coco Republic, GlobeWest desktop) make you scroll back up.
  • On phones every store uses one sheet with all facets, and the good ones put a live result count on the button. None of them puts the Filter bar at the bottom, but the bottom is where the thumb is, and our chat button already sits 88 px up, leaving room for a bottom bar.
  • Stock first: West Elm and Pottery Barn lead with availability; trade buyers (GlobeWest) see stock and size on the card.

What is wrong

  1. Two ways to filter that behave differently: quick chips apply at once, the drawer needs "Show N products". Buyers do not know which one they are using.
  2. The quick chips are values with no facet name. "Noa Desert" or "Dining Table" read like links, not filters, and on the phone only the first one and a half are visible.
  3. On desktop the drawer covers the grid you are filtering, and only the first facet is open: one extra click for every facet.
  4. Sort and the product count are not in the sticky bar. Changing the sort after scrolling means going back to the top.
  5. The applied-filter chips are not sticky. After scrolling only quick values show their state.
  6. After applying, the page reloads at the top (breadcrumbs, title, trade notice), not at the results.
  7. Phone: the bar sits at the top edge, far from the thumb. The drawer slides in from the left like a menu, not up from the bottom like a sheet.
  8. Width / Depth are behind closed accordions, although size is one of the first things a retailer filters by.

Keep

  • Live count on the apply button, counts next to every option, From / To inputs with the "In this list 34-240" hint, multi-select, chips with Clear all, works without JS, noindex + clean canonical, focus trap and Esc.

Data, not this ticket

  • On local data colour_group holds product colour names (5 values, 1 product each) and material holds composition text ("High density marble with premium marble film, MDF, Solid Ash Solid Ribbing"). Any layout looks poor with these until the PIM fills real groups.
